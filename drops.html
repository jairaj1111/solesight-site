How it's built

An end-to-end system: nine live data feeds, one autonomous nightly pipeline, zero servers — the platform publishes itself while its builder sleeps.

01

Collect

Google Trends, eBay (US · UK · DE), Bluesky, Mastodon, YouTube, Google News + sneaker press, Wikipedia and 19 boutique feeds — every adapter degrades gracefully if a source refuses.

02

Store

One SQLite database, saved after every nightly run — with the previous night's copy and weekly snapshots kept as backups.

03

Understand

A RoBERTa transformer scores community sentiment; Prophet fits a 30-day demand forecast with confidence bands per model.

04

Detect

Launch Radar flags demand spikes ≥3× each model's trailing baseline and labels every lifecycle stage.

05

Score & explain

Five weighted signals blend into a 0–100 Hype Score, plus a plain-English insight per model.

06

Publish

Everything lands in one static data.json; GitHub Actions redeploys this site — no human in the loop.

Tool choices, briefly: Prophet because sneaker demand is seasonal and per-model history is short · RoBERTa because community chatter is noisy short-form text · SQLite + one static JSON because zero infrastructure is a feature, not a compromise.

How the score works

Every shoe gets one number from 0 to 100. A 99 means it's hotter right now than 99% of every score we've ever recorded. Higher means hotter. That's it.

1

We check 5 things, every night

What it resells for, whether Google searches are rising, how many people search for it, how much people post about it, and whether they talk about it positively.

2

We blend them into one number

Resale counts the most, because real money is the strongest signal. If a shoe is missing one of the five, it's scored on the rest instead of getting a zero.

3

We compare it to every score ever

That number is ranked against every daily score we've recorded since July 2026, so the hottest shoes land in the 90s and a 90 means the same thing every night.

The 5 things, and how much each counts

For the nerds — the full formula, every data source & a worked example

The formula

raw   = Σ(wᵢ · vᵢ) / Σ(wᵢ)   over available signals
score = % of all recorded daily raw scores below it

Why the second line: no shoe maxes all five signals at once, so raw scores bunch low — the hottest shoe in the market sits near 50 raw. The published Hype Score is that raw score's rank against every daily score recorded since July 2026, frozen as a fixed reference, so 99 means hotter than 99% of all readings and a number means the same thing every night. Rankings never change — only the scale.

Every component maps onto 0–100 before weighting:

  • Search intensity — Google's 0–100 index, 14-day mean. Relative to each model's own peak, so it measures how hot the model is vs. itself, not raw volume across models.
  • Search momentum — recent 14d vs. prior 14d percent change, mapped as 50 + Δ% × 0.8, clamped to [0, 100]. Saturates at roughly ±62%. Skipped when the prior two weeks average under 5 on Google's scale — a percent change off a baseline that thin is noise, not signal; those models are scored on their other signals.
  • Resale premium — median price ÷ retail MSRP, mapped linearly from 0.8× (→ 0) to 2.6× (→ 100). The clamp caps extreme listings; prices are decile-trimmed before the median to drop fakes and typo listings. A model with no known retail price contributes nothing (see missing signals).
  • Social buzz — daily cross-platform engagement, normalized 0–100 to the model's own peak (same convention as Trends).
  • Community mood — per-post transformer sentiment P(pos) − P(neg) ∈ [−1, 1], averaged over all scored posts, mapped to 0–100.

Missing signals: weights renormalize over whatever is present — a model missing resale data is scored fairly on the rest, never implicitly zeroed. Update cadence: search & forecasts nightly (stalest-first); other signals refresh whenever their source runs. Baseline window: each model carries ~269 days of daily history.

What the score can't see (yet)

  • Resale uses asking prices on eBay, not final sale prices — sold-price data is only available through paid partnerships.
  • Google search numbers show how hot a shoe is compared with its own history, not how many people search in total.
  • Mood and buzz come from YouTube, Bluesky and Mastodon. Instagram and TikTok aren't included — there's no free way to get their data.
  • Wikipedia views, press coverage and boutique sell-outs show on each shoe's page but don't count toward the score until we've tested that they make it more accurate.
